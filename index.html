5 GST Filing Mistakes Businesses Should Avoid

Common, costly errors that trigger penalties, blocked credit and GST notices — and exactly how to prevent them.

6 min read Updated July 2026 Biznexco Editorial Team
AT

Written by the Biznexco Editorial Team

GST & Compliance Advisory, Biznexco Solutions

Goods and Services Tax compliance sits at the centre of every registered business's monthly routine, yet it remains one of the most error-prone parts of running a company. A single missed deadline or mismatched invoice can trigger interest, penalties, blocked input tax credit, or a formal GST notice that eats up valuable time.

In this article, we break down the five GST filing mistakes we see most often at Biznexco Solutions, along with practical, actionable tips your finance team can start applying today.

Table of Contents

  1. 01 Missing GST Filing Deadlines
  2. 02 Incorrect GSTIN Details
  3. 03 Wrong Input Tax Credit Claims
  4. 04 Invoice Errors
  5. 05 Poor Bookkeeping
  6. Summary
  7. FAQs
01

Missing GST Filing Deadlines

GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B have fixed due dates every month (or quarter under QRMP), and missing them is one of the most common — and most expensive — mistakes businesses make. Late filing attracts interest on unpaid tax plus a per-day late fee, and it can also delay your GST returns being reflected in your buyers' input tax credit, straining vendor relationships.

Practical Tip

Maintain a GST compliance calendar with automated reminders 5–7 days before every due date, and assign a single owner responsible for filing each return on time.

02

Incorrect GSTIN Details

Entering a wrong or outdated GSTIN on an invoice or return — for your own business or a vendor/customer — is a small typo with big consequences. It can cause invoices to be rejected in GSTR-2B reconciliation, delay input tax credit for your buyer, and create mismatches that surface later as GST notices.

Practical Tip

Verify GSTIN and legal name on the GST portal before onboarding any new vendor or customer, and re-verify annually since registration status can change.

03

Wrong Input Tax Credit Claims

Claiming ITC on ineligible expenses, claiming credit that doesn't appear in GSTR-2B, or simply over-claiming due to poor reconciliation are frequent triggers for GST audits and demand notices. Under-claiming is just as costly, since it means paying more tax than necessary.

Practical Tip

Reconcile your purchase register against GSTR-2B every month before filing GSTR-3B, and only claim ITC that is clearly reflected and eligible under GST law.

04

Invoice Errors

Wrong HSN/SAC codes, incorrect tax rates, missing mandatory fields, or duplicate/skipped invoice numbers are surprisingly common — especially for businesses issuing a high volume of invoices manually. These errors ripple into your GSTR-1 filing and can cause credit mismatches for your customers.

Practical Tip

Use GST-compliant billing software with pre-set HSN codes and tax rates, and run a monthly invoice audit to catch duplicates or gaps in your invoice sequence.

05

Poor Bookkeeping

GST filing is only as accurate as the books behind it. Delayed data entry, unreconciled bank transactions, and disorganised purchase/sales records make it nearly impossible to file correctly — forcing rushed, error-prone returns at the deadline every single month.

Practical Tip

Keep books updated weekly rather than monthly, and reconcile bank statements continuously so GST data is always audit-ready, not assembled last minute.

Summary

Most GST penalties are avoidable. Missed deadlines, incorrect GSTIN details, faulty ITC claims, invoice errors and poor bookkeeping account for the majority of notices businesses receive — and every one of them can be prevented with a disciplined monthly process and the right compliance partner.

  • File on a fixed monthly calendar, never at the last minute
  • Verify GSTIN and reconcile ITC before every filing
  • Keep books current, not reconstructed after the fact

Frequently Asked Questions

Most regular taxpayers file GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B monthly, while eligible small taxpayers can opt for the QRMP scheme and file quarterly with monthly tax payments.

Late filing attracts late fees and interest on unpaid tax, and repeated delays can affect e-way bill generation and your customers' input tax credit.

Some corrections can be made in the following period's return, but certain errors require formal amendment requests. Fixing details before filing is always safer and faster.

GST Registration Checklist

Everything you need to register for GST — organised so you can gather it in one sitting.

Tip: click "Download / Save as PDF" above, then choose "Save as PDF" as the destination printer to keep a copy on your device.

1. Identity & Business Constitution Documents

  • PAN Card of the business / proprietor / partners / directors
  • Aadhaar Card of proprietor / partners / directors
  • Passport-size photograph of proprietor / partners / directors
  • Certificate of Incorporation (for Companies/LLPs) If applicable
  • Partnership Deed If applicable
  • MOA & AOA (for Companies) If applicable

2. Address Proof

  • Latest electricity / water / property tax bill of the business premises
  • Rent agreement (if premises is rented)
  • No Objection Certificate (NOC) from the property owner (if rented)
  • Property ownership documents (if self-owned)

3. Bank Account Details

  • Cancelled cheque of the business bank account
  • Bank statement or first page of the passbook
  • IFSC code and account number

4. Authorization Documents

  • Letter of Authorisation / Board Resolution for the authorised signatory
  • Digital Signature Certificate (DSC) of the authorised signatory Companies/LLPs

5. Business Details

  • Nature of business activity and HSN/SAC codes of goods or services
  • Business commencement date
  • Estimated annual turnover
  • Details of additional business places, if any

Before You Apply

  • Confirm your turnover crosses the applicable registration threshold, or that you want to register voluntarily
  • Keep scanned copies (PDF/JPEG) of all documents ready for upload on the GST portal
  • Have your registered mobile number and email ID accessible for OTP verification

ITR Document Checklist

A simple checklist to prepare for hassle-free Income Tax Return filing.

Tip: click "Download / Save as PDF" above, then choose "Save as PDF" as the destination printer to keep a copy on your device.

1. Basic Identity Documents

  • PAN Card
  • Aadhaar Card
  • Bank account details (account number & IFSC) for refund credit

2. Income Documents — Salaried Individuals

  • Form 16 from employer(s)
  • Salary slips (if Form 16 unavailable)
  • Form 26AS / Annual Information Statement (AIS)
  • Details of any other employer income during the year

3. Income Documents — Business & Professionals

  • Profit & Loss statement and Balance Sheet
  • Books of accounts / bank statements for the financial year
  • GST returns filed during the year, if registered If applicable
  • Details of professional receipts and expenses

4. Other Income

  • Interest certificates from banks / post office (savings, FD, RD)
  • Rental income details and lease agreement, if any
  • Capital gains statements — shares, mutual funds, property sale
  • Dividend income statements

5. Deductions & Exemptions (Old Regime)

  • Section 80C — LIC, PPF, ELSS, tuition fees, home loan principal receipts
  • Section 80D — health insurance premium receipts
  • Home loan interest certificate — Section 24(b)
  • Donation receipts eligible under Section 80G
  • HRA proof — rent receipts and landlord PAN (if rent exceeds ₹1,00,000/year)

6. Tax Already Paid

  • TDS certificates (Form 16 / Form 16A)
  • Advance tax / self-assessment tax challans, if paid
  • TCS certificates, if applicable

Before You File

  • Reconcile all income and TDS figures with Form 26AS / AIS
  • Confirm the correct ITR form applicable to your income sources
  • Keep your Aadhaar-linked mobile number active for e-verification

A Simple Guide to UAE VAT

A practical reference for businesses on registration, filing and compliance.

This guide provides a simple overview of UAE VAT. Always refer to the latest Federal Tax Authority (FTA) regulations for current rules.

1. What is VAT?

  • VAT stands for Value Added Tax.
  • The standard VAT rate in the UAE is 5%.
  • VAT applies to most goods and services.
  • Registered businesses collect VAT and remit it to the Federal Tax Authority.

2. VAT Registration

  • Mandatory registration applies once the prescribed turnover threshold is exceeded.
  • Eligible businesses below the threshold may register voluntarily.

3. Types of VAT Supplies

Standard Rated
Taxed at the standard 5% rate.
Zero Rated
Taxed at 0%, but still reportable.
Exempt
Not subject to VAT at all.

4. Output VAT & Input VAT

Output VAT is the VAT charged on taxable sales. Input VAT is the VAT paid on eligible business purchases. VAT payable is determined after adjusting eligible input VAT against output VAT.

5. Tax Invoice Requirements

  • Supplier details
  • Tax Registration Number (TRN)
  • Invoice number and date
  • Description of goods or services
  • Taxable amount, VAT amount and total amount

6. VAT Return Filing

  • File VAT returns for each assigned tax period.
  • Submit the return before the due date.
  • Pay any VAT due on time to avoid penalties.

7. Records to Maintain

  • Sales and purchase invoices
  • Credit notes and debit notes
  • Import / export documentation
  • Accounting records and filed VAT returns
  • Supporting documents for all transactions

8. Common VAT Mistakes

  • Late registration
  • Incorrect VAT calculations
  • Incomplete tax invoices
  • Incorrect input VAT claims
  • Late filing or late payment
  • Poor record keeping

9. Best Practices

  • Maintain accurate, up-to-date records.
  • Reconcile VAT regularly, not just before filing.
  • Issue fully compliant tax invoices.
  • File returns and make payments on time.
  • Keep supporting documents readily accessible.
  • Stay updated with the latest FTA guidance.
